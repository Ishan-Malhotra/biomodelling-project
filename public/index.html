Enter φ, ψ and ω for each residue, plus the position of its carbonyl oxygen (C=O length, CA–C–O angle and N–CA–C–O dihedral). The backbone atoms N, CA, C and O are then built. The remaining bond lengths and angles use ideal values (Engh & Huber): N–CA 1.458 Å, CA–C 1.525 Å, C–N 1.329 Å; N–CA–C 111.2°, CA–C–N 116.2°, C–N–CA 121.7°.

Residue 1 has no φ and the last residue has no ω, because those angles need atoms from a neighbouring residue. Leave N–CA–C–O blank to use ψ + 180°, which keeps O in the peptide plane.
